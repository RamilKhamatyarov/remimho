# Paddle rectangle sweep and centre aiming

After [0007](0007-paddle-contact-and-goal-resolution.md) closed the tunnelling and scoring holes, the puck still passed through the paddle from the opening serve onward, for the human paddle and the bot paddle alike. The puck was seen entering the paddle rectangle and continuing out the back into the goal, so this is a distinct defect rather than a regression of the earlier fix. Three causes combined.

**Collision tested only the face plane.** `paddleContact` asked one question: does the puck reach the vertical face, and is it within the paddle's vertical span at that instant? A puck that cleared the face plane just above or below the paddle was declared a miss and kept its full travel. Because the puck is still moving vertically after that instant — the serve leaves at `vy = 200` and spin curves it further — it could clear the face above the paddle at the crossing and then descend into the paddle rectangle over the remaining twenty pixels of horizontal travel. Nothing tested that later part of the path, so the puck finished the tick inside the paddle body and carried on into the goal. This is the pass-through that was actually observed, it is independent of puck speed, and it hits both paddles equally, which is why the bot conceded the same way.

The fix replaces the face-plane test with a swept circle against the whole paddle rectangle. `paddleImpact` expands the rectangle by the puck radius and clips the tick's travel against it with the slab method, yielding both the entry time and which axis was crossed first. A face entry keeps the existing bounce, angle and spin behaviour. A top or bottom entry is a new `resolveEdge` outcome: the puck is placed on that edge, its vertical velocity is inverted, and a paddle touch is recorded so the goal veto from 0007 still applies. Entry times that tie resolve as a face hit, which keeps corner contacts behaving like the face. The test remains `O(1)` and needs no sub-stepping, and because the puck is always placed on the boundary it can never end a tick inside the paddle. The arrival height is still mirrored back into the playfield first so a puck that rebounds off a wall inside the same tick is tested at its true height.

**The paddle was aimed by its top edge.** The client sent the raw cursor Y and the reducer stored it directly as `paddle2Y`, which is the paddle's top. The paddle therefore rendered half its height below the pointer, and a player aiming at the puck met it with the extreme top edge, where the bounce angle is at its maximum and a few pixels of drift became a clean miss. `GameAction.MovePaddle.y` is now the requested paddle **centre**, and the reducer subtracts half the paddle height before clamping. The web client needs no change: the cursor Y it already sends now centres the paddle. `BotController` keeps its internal top-edge arithmetic and converts once when it emits, and `DemoGifGenerator` was re-based so its recorded sweep is unchanged.

**The bot could be out-run.** `FIXED_PADDLE_SPEED` capped the bot paddle at `260` px/s while the puck serves at `300` px/s horizontally and accelerates with `progressiveSpeed` over the match, so the bot frequently conceded because it physically could not arrive, not because of its configured aim error. Paddle speed now derives from skill and from the puck itself: `BASE_PADDLE_SPEED` plus an aggression bonus, raised to at least `1.15 ×` the puck's vertical speed and capped at `MAX_PADDLE_SPEED`. Difficulty is still carried by reaction delay, aim error and prediction depth, which is where [0006](0006-bot-difficulty-scaling.md) intends it to live.

Treating `MovePaddle.y` as a centre changes the meaning of a recorded field, so replays captured before this change place paddles half a paddle height low, and the rectangle sweep makes contact results diverge from earlier recordings. The protobuf schema is untouched and `ReplayConverter` needs no migration. Regression coverage lives in `PaddleContactTest`: `puck curving in over the paddle edge rebounds instead of entering the body` reproduces the reported pass-through and fails against the 0007 collision model, `puck is never left inside the paddle rectangle` pins the invariant, and `move paddle positions the paddle centre on the requested y` pins the aiming contract. The earlier `puck outside paddle at crossing is not caught` case asserted the defective behaviour — its puck genuinely clips the paddle's top corner — so it was retargeted to a puck that passes clear and kept as the negative control.
