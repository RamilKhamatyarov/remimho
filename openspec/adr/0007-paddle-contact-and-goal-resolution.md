# Paddle contact and goal resolution

A puck aimed at the middle of a paddle was sometimes not deflected, and a goal was awarded instead. The symptom had six independent causes in `PaddlePhysics`, `TickReducer`, and `MviReducer`; fixing any one of them alone still left the bug reachable through the others.

**Direction gate excluded a resting puck.** `movesToward` required strictly `vx < 0` for side A and `vx > 0` for side B, so a puck whose horizontal velocity had reached exactly zero matched neither side and was never treated as contact. The magnet power-up can cancel `vx` by accelerating the puck toward the paddle centre, leaving it parked inside the paddle with no way out. `separatingFrom` replaces the gate and skips only a puck that is actively leaving the face, so zero horizontal velocity now counts as contact.

**The contact test had an escape window.** Contact required either `crossesPaddleFace`, which demanded the previous centre be in front of the face, or `overlapsPaddleFace`, which stopped being true once the centre passed `x < -radius`. A puck that began a tick already behind the face satisfied neither branch and slipped through untouched. A single `reachedFace` check — the advanced centre is at or beyond the face — replaces both, so a puck behind the face keeps registering contact until it is deflected or genuinely misses in Y.

**The sweep basis was corrupted by the wall bounce.** Paddle resolution ran after `PuckPhysics.resolveWalls`, yet interpolated the contact point between the pre-bounce start and the post-bounce end. For a fast diagonal puck that struck a wall and reached the paddle within one tick, that straight line produced a contact Y far from the true one, `overlapsY` reported a miss, and a goal followed. Paddles now resolve before walls so the sweep spans the uncorrupted path, and `reflectIntoField` mirrors the swept Y back inside the playfield so a same-tick wall rebound still lands on the paddle.

**Scoring was purely geometric.** `resolveScore` looked only at `puck.x` and ran after `resolveLines`, which can rebound or teleport the puck. A puck the paddle had already deflected could be relocated across the goal line and counted as conceded. `defendedThisTick` now vetoes the goal when the defending paddle registered a touch carrying the same `elapsedNs`, which is the invariant that no goal may be scored if that paddle contacted the puck during the same tick. The veto is scoped to the defending side so a legitimate goal at the far end still counts.

**Paddle travel was lost between ticks.** `movePaddle` overwrote `paddleVelocity` with the newest delta, so when several `MovePaddle` actions arrived inside one frame only the last survived, and the tick then zeroed it. Bounce angle and spin therefore under-reported how far the paddle had actually moved. The deltas now accumulate and the tick zeroes them once it has consumed them.

**A freed puck had no usable ejection speed.** `redirect` coerced the outgoing speed to `MIN_PUCK_SPEED` of `1.0`, so a puck released from the dead zone crawled off the face at under half a pixel per second and was immediately caught again. `ejectionSpeed` gives any puck below `MIN_CONTACT_SPEED` a `MIN_EJECT_SPEED` of `120.0`, so it visibly leaves the paddle.

The swept test is continuous and `O(1)`: it intersects the puck's travel segment with the paddle face rather than sampling positions, so correctness does not depend on puck speed and no sub-stepping is needed.

These changes alter tick ordering and contact results, so a match replayed from a log recorded before this change can diverge. The protobuf replay schema is untouched, so existing replay files remain readable and `ReplayConverter` needs no migration. Regression coverage in `PaddleContactTest` and `PaddleContactIntegrationTest` pins each cause above: a fast puck at the centre of a stationary paddle, a near-zero-velocity puck inside the paddle, a puck crossing the face within one tick, goal resolution after all collisions, and a paddle moved in the same tick.
